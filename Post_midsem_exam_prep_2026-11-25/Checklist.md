Follow [Study_flow.md](Study_flow.md) in order. Assignment links open [the complete Markdown conversion](MfoxAssignSamar.md). All boxes start unchecked.

**Completion rule:** tick a concept after you can explain its definition/result, state relevant conditions, and solve a representative question without notes. Tick a practice item after attempting it and correcting your mistakes.

**Coverage labels:** “Lecture” means recorded in the class summary or inspected handwritten notes. “Assignment” means explicit in the supplied compendium. “Refresh” means already in the pre-midsem PDF. Supporting methods are included where the exercises require them; they do not imply that an entire textbook chapter is examinable.

## 1. Proof by cases, strong induction and well-ordering

Lecture source: Lectures 1–4, 5–8 October; Tutorial 7, 12 October.

- [ ] Recognize invalid “proof by sampling,” “proof by bullying” and “proof by insulting.”
- [ ] Proof by cases: cases must cover every possibility.
- [ ] Ramsey numbers and the case proof of $R(3,3)=6$; understand the discussion of larger diagonal Ramsey numbers.
- [ ] Strong induction: canonical structure, hypothesis covering earlier cases, and multiple base cases where needed.
- [ ] Choose between ordinary and strong induction and explain the choice.
- [ ] Prove existence of prime factorization by strong induction.
- [ ] Prove every integer weight at least 8 kg can be formed from 3 kg and 5 kg weights.
- [ ] Find which group sizes can be split into teams of 4 or 7 and prove the answer by strong induction — [A2 Q1](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a2-q1).
- [ ] Prove $n\le3^{n/3}$ for every integer $n\ge0$ by strong induction — [A2 Q2](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a2-q2).
- [ ] State the well-ordering principle for the nonnegative integers.
- [ ] Use the least-counterexample proof template correctly.
- [ ] Prove existence of prime factorization using WOP.
- [ ] Prove the sum formula for the first $n$ nonnegative integers using WOP, with the indexing stated explicitly.
- [ ] Apply WOP/least-counterexample descent to prove $4a^3+2b^3=c^3$ has no positive-integer solution — [A1 Q5(a)](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a1-q5).
- [ ] Explain equivalence of ordinary induction, strong induction and WOP.
- [ ] Convert a proof between those methods and compare their advantages.
- [ ] Practice: [Assignment 1](MfoxAssignSamar.md#assignment-1), selecting the proof/induction/WOP questions; classify ordinary-proof questions as refresh.
- [ ] Practice: [Assignment 2 Q1–Q2](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a2-q1); these are strong induction despite the contents-page logic label.

## 2. Recursive data types, functions and structural induction

Lecture source: Lectures 5–7, 13–15 October; Tutorial 8, 2 November.

- [ ] Recursive data types: base elements, constructors and the restriction to elements generated by those rules.
- [ ] Examples: nonnegative integers, character strings, binary trees, bracket strings, arithmetic expressions and two-player complete-information games.
- [ ] Write recursive definitions of sets and justify that they generate exactly the intended elements — [A4 Q2](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a4-q2).
- [ ] Define functions recursively over a data type; identify ill-formed definitions and distinguish established termination from conjectured termination.
- [ ] Structural induction: a base proof and one induction step for each constructor.
- [ ] Character strings as a recursive type; empty string, constructors, concatenation and length.
- [ ] Define string reversal recursively and prove reversal of concatenation — [A4 Q1](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a4-q1).
- [ ] Prove associativity of concatenation by structural induction.
- [ ] Define character-count recursively and prove additivity under concatenation — [A4 Q3](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a4-q3).
- [ ] Define the arithmetic-expression type `Aexp` and its constructors.
- [ ] Define and trace recursive evaluation of arithmetic expressions.
- [ ] Define and trace recursive substitution in arithmetic expressions.
- [ ] Compare substitution and environment evaluation models, including their advantages and disadvantages.
- [ ] Prove the evaluation models equivalent by structural induction.
- [ ] Context: Ackermann's definition and growth, Collatz recursion and its termination question.
- [ ] Context: Knuth up-arrows; successor, addition, multiplication, exponentiation, tetration, pentation and hexation.
- [ ] Context: Graham's number and the class discussion of lower bounds on TREE(3).
- [ ] Practice: complete [Assignment 4 Q1–Q3](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a4-q1) and Tutorial 8 Q1–Q3.

## 3. Explicit bijections and basic counting

Lecture source: Lectures 8–9, 21–22 October.

- [ ] Refresh the arrow interpretation of total, functional, injective, surjective and bijective relations.
- [ ] Relate injections, surjections and bijections to cardinality; distinguish finite-set conclusions from their infinite-set versions.
- [ ] Cantor pairing: encode pairs of nonnegative integers and understand its role in enumerating rationals.
- [ ] Stern–Brocot tree: enumerate positive rationals; extend to zero and negative rationals when describing all of $\mathbb Q$.
- [ ] Bijection rule: translate the original objects into objects that are easier to count.
- [ ] Encode selections as bit strings; distinguish labelled positions from indistinguishable selections.
- [ ] Stars and bars: 12 ice-creams from 5 flavours correspond to 16-bit strings with four separators, giving $\binom{16}{4}$.
- [ ] Explain why $5^{12}$ counts a different, ordered version of that ice-cream problem.
- [ ] Sum rule for disjoint cases.
- [ ] Product rule and generalized product rule.
- [ ] Division rule via a $k$-to-1 correspondence; verify that every object has the same number of representations.
- [ ] Count ordered versus unordered meals, passwords of different lengths, circular seating and right/down grid paths.
- [ ] Binomial coefficients, subset counting and the binomial theorem.
- [ ] Multinomial coefficients and the multinomial theorem.
- [ ] Practice: [A5 Q2](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a5-q2) nonadjacent book choices via a bijection; [A5 Q3](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a5-q3) three-number selections by residue classes.
- [ ] Practice: Tutorial 8 Q4–Q6.

## 5. Integer functions, summations, products and asymptotics

Lecture source: Lectures 11–13, 27–29 October; asymptotic applications in Lecture 16. Assignment 4 Q4–Q5/Q7–Q8 and Assignment 5 Q8–Q9 supply explicit practice.

Floor/ceiling/modulo are studied before the sums that use them, although the lectures introduced some summation methods first.

- [ ] Floor and ceiling definitions, defining inequalities and integer-shift/negation properties.
- [ ] Modulo definition and its connection to floor; state the divisor and sign convention when using negative inputs.
- [ ] Solve floor/ceiling identities and inequalities by tracking fractional parts/cases — [A4 Q7](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a4-q7), [Q8](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a4-q8).
- [ ] Evaluate finite sums, including geometric and weighted sums; use the perturbation method where appropriate.
- [ ] Evaluate the alternating sums — [A4 Q4](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a4-q4).
- [ ] Evaluate the rational alternating sum — [A4 Q5](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a4-q5).
- [ ] Derive upper and lower bounds for sums, including integral comparisons with monotonicity and endpoints stated.
- [ ] Bound the sum of square roots of the first $n$ nonnegative integers.
- [ ] Harmonic numbers: definition, estimates and growth.
- [ ] Multiple sums: draw/identify the index region and change summation order.
- [ ] Evaluate the sum of the first $n$ harmonic numbers by exchanging summations.
- [ ] Iverson brackets: encode conditions as 0/1 and manipulate summation limits.
- [ ] Obtain the closed form for the sum of floors of square roots, with the index range stated.
- [ ] Use that floor sum to derive the improved lower bound on the square-root sum.
- [ ] Products: convert positive products to logarithmic sums to obtain estimates.
- [ ] Supporting factorial estimates, including Stirling's approximation when used in the notes/textbook; distinguish bounds from asymptotic equivalence.
- [ ] Define $O$, $\Omega$, $\Theta$ and asymptotic equivalence $\sim$; state domain/sign assumptions.
- [ ] Prove/disprove factorial comparisons — [A5 Q8](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a5-q8).
- [ ] Find Big-O witnesses $c,n_0$, including the smallest requested positive-integer witnesses — [A5 Q9](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a5-q9).
- [ ] Practice: complete the cited Assignment 4/5 questions; revisit the class-summary Tutorial 8 discussion of integer functions using the lecture examples. The supplied Tutorial 8 sheet has no separate integer-function exercise.

## 6. Modelling recurrences and solving linear recurrences

Lecture source: Lectures 14–15, 3–4 November; Tutorial 9, 12 November.

- [ ] Translate a recursive problem/algorithm into a recurrence with sufficient initial conditions.
- [ ] Josephus problem: derive its recurrences and closed form; specify whether survivors are numbered from 0 or 1.
- [ ] Guess-and-verify: calculate examples, propose a formula, then prove it by induction.
- [ ] Expansion/plug-and-chug: unfold the recurrence, identify the depth and evaluate the resulting sum.
- [ ] Towers of Hanoi: derive the recurrence by matching lower and upper bounds; solve and verify it.
- [ ] Merge sort: derive and solve its recurrence, specifying the cost model and input-size convention.
- [ ] Homogeneous linear recurrences with constant coefficients: characteristic polynomial and distinct roots.
- [ ] Repeated characteristic roots and their polynomial factors.
- [ ] Use initial conditions to determine the constants in the general solution.
- [ ] Fibonacci recurrence and its closed-form solution.
- [ ] Nonhomogeneous linear recurrences: homogeneous plus particular solution.
- [ ] Choose a particular-solution form and handle overlap with homogeneous roots.
- [ ] Modified Hanoi with a disk-size-dependent movement cost.
- [ ] Verify proposed solutions by substitution and checking initial conditions.
- [ ] Practice: lecture Josephus/Fibonacci/Hanoi/merge-sort examples; the linear recurrences in Tutorial 9 Q4–Q5 will be revisited using generating functions in Stage 8.

## 7. Divide-and-conquer recurrences

Lecture source: Lecture 16, 5 November; Tutorial 9, 12 November. Practice: [Assignment 6 Q1](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a6-q1).

- [ ] Identify the number of recursive calls, subproblem fractions and work outside recursive calls.
- [ ] Akra–Bazzi: state the applicable theorem, including coefficient, shrinkage, perturbation and growth assumptions.
- [ ] Identify $a_i,b_i,h_i(n)$ and $g(n)$ in each recurrence.
- [ ] Find $p$ from $\sum_i a_i b_i^p=1$.
- [ ] Evaluate $\Theta\!\left(n^p\left(1+\int_1^n g(u)/u^{p+1}\,du\right)\right)$ under the theorem's conditions.
- [ ] Explain why suitable floor/ceiling perturbations and base conditions preserve the asymptotic order; check the conditions before using this claim.
- [ ] Master theorem as the equal-subproblem-size corollary; identify each case and its hypotheses.
- [ ] Recognize when the standard Master theorem does not apply and use a suitable method.
- [ ] Compare the effects of changing the number and sizes of subproblems.
- [ ] Practice: [A6 Q1(a)](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a6-q1) equal subproblems; Q1(b) unequal subproblems; Q1(c) perturbed sizes. Use applicable methods and distinguish exact special-input formulas from asymptotic bounds.

- [ ] Core series: geometric, scaled geometric, finite geometric, $(1+x)^m$ and $(1-ax)^{-k}$.
- [ ] Model unrestricted, bounded, even/multiple and optional selections with the appropriate factor.
- [ ] Shifting, scaling, addition and multiplication of generating functions; track starting indices.
- [ ] Product/convolution rule and its proof from coefficient multiplication.
- [ ] Counting applications, including deriving the binomial theorem and the lecture's “weird” quiz problem.
- [ ] Prefix-sum generating function $A(x)/(1-x)$ and the sums-of-squares exercise — [A6 Q2](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a6-q2).
- [ ] Rational generating functions: write the quotient of polynomials.
- [ ] Partial fractions with distinct roots and coefficient recovery.
- [ ] Partial fractions with repeated roots and coefficient recovery.
- [ ] Model Super Sam's mishaps and obtain a closed form — [A6 Q3](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a6-q3).
- [ ] Solve homogeneous linear recurrences by generating functions, including Fibonacci.
- [ ] Solve nonhomogeneous linear recurrences by generating functions, including Hanoi.
- [ ] Include initial-condition terms when shifting recurrence sums — [A6 Q4](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a6-q4), [Q5](MfoxAssignSamar.md#a6-q5).
- [ ] Formal power series: justify algebraic manipulations without assuming numerical convergence.
- [ ] Factorial and $n\,n!$ coefficient examples; derive the lecture's iterative factorial identity.

## 9. Graph fundamentals and coloring

Source: [handwritten notes](../Study_material_after_mid_sems/MFOCS_Notes_Aug-Nov_2026.pdf), PDF pp. 219 and 221, inspected visually; Assignment 7 supplies applications. The class summary's final lecture entry is blank. These note pages do not show dates, so no exact lecture date is assigned here.

- [ ] Simple undirected graphs versus directed graphs; vertices, edges, adjacency and degree.
- [ ] Handshaking theorem $\sum_v\deg(v)=2|E|$ and the even-number-of-odd-degree-vertices corollary.
- [ ] Bipartition degree totals and average-degree relation, with nonempty parts and edges crossing the partition.
- [ ] Standard graphs: complete graphs $K_n$, path graphs and cycles $C_n$; count their vertices/edges/degrees.
- [ ] Graph isomorphism: a bijection preserving adjacency in both directions.
- [ ] Prove isomorphism is an equivalence relation; use invariants to rule it out and an explicit bijection to establish it.
- [ ] Proper vertex coloring, $k$-colorability and chromatic number $\chi(G)$.
- [ ] Chromatic numbers of complete graphs and even/odd cycles.
- [ ] Bipartite graphs and 2-colorability; distinguish edgeless cases from graphs containing an edge.
- [ ] Prove the maximum-degree bound $\chi(G)\le\Delta(G)+1$ by induction on the number of vertices.
- [ ] Context: graph-coloring complexity, SAT, and distributed/online coloring; distinguish easy 2-coloring from fixed $k\ge3$ coloring.
